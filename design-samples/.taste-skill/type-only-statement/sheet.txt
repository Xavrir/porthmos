This is the direction for this build. Adopt it. Do not blend it with the layout you would have made anyway,
and do not redraw because it is unfamiliar: unfamiliar is the point. Redraw a slot only when it conflicts
with a stated brand constraint or needs an asset that does not exist, and say why in your report.

The parts of this sheet were written separately and will disagree in small ways. Settle every disagreement in this order:
**house rules** (including type no smaller than 13px and body text of 16px or more) → **the direction** (the idea, the composition habits, where the
one accent goes) → **the type system and the palette** (which face does which job, the five size steps, which colour is the ground and where the
accent goes) → **the hero blueprint** → **the section blueprints**. Under each blueprint below, "Adjusted for this page" lists where it disagrees.
In practice: take geometry from blueprints (columns, proportions, what sits where) and take everything else from above them. Map every size a
blueprint names onto one of your five type steps instead of adding a sixth. Use one button shape, one corner radius and one pair of page margins on
the whole page, whatever individual blueprints say. If a blueprint names a kind of typeface (a light serif, a heavy grotesk), use the dealt face.

## 1. The idea
The first screen is made only of words, colour and space, good enough that nobody misses a picture there. Pictures arrive later, large and few, as relief from the type. This is the honest answer when a subject has nothing worth photographing: instead of inventing illustrations, set what you have to say as well as it can be set. The typography carries a point of view through scale (one enormous statement, everything else small), through measure (a narrow, bookish column or a full-width line), and through one committed colour.

Before anything else, write one sentence in your notes: "On this page, <the subject's own material> becomes <the device>."
Fill the first blank from your inventory of what only this client has (its name and what it means, its street, its numbers, its
constraint), then put the nearest competitor in the sentence: if it still works, the idea is the category's, not the client's.
Then name the one live thing the visitor can do on the page (SKILL.md step 4).

## 2. Composition
First screen: the statement, two lines at most, set at 9–14vw and occupying the full width with tight leading, positioned in the upper two-thirds. One supporting sentence at 20–24px sits bottom-left on the same margin and the single action sits bottom-right on the same baseline. No navigation bar; two or three links in a top corner. Sections that follow are typographic set pieces, each different: a list of five to eight capabilities set at 4–6vw one per line; a paragraph at 28–36px in a 28ch column pushed to the right half; a pair of large numerals with a sentence each; a single quotation filling a colour field. Each section uses a different horizontal position (left, right, full) so the page has movement without imagery.

## 3. Type — Erode
A serif with chiselled, angular curves and wedge serifs, sharp at display size and firm in text. Suits studios, galleries, advisory firms and written guides.
```html
<link rel="preconnect" href="https://api.fontshare.com">
<link rel="preconnect" href="https://cdn.fontshare.com" crossorigin>
<link href="https://api.fontshare.com/v2/css?f[]=erode@400,500,600&display=swap" rel="stylesheet">
```
- Display: **Erode** — weight 500 · tracking -0.025em · leading 0.95 · sentence case, or tight capitals at 32–40px for a single statement
- Text: **Erode** — 400 for body, 600 for emphasis, italic for titles of works · leading 1.5 · tracking 0
```css
:root {
  --step-small: clamp(0.8125rem, 0.79rem + 0.1vw, 0.875rem);
  --step-body: clamp(1.0625rem, 1rem + 0.25vw, 1.1875rem);
  --step-lead: clamp(1.25rem, 1.1rem + 0.7vw, 1.625rem);
  --step-head: clamp(2rem, 1.3rem + 3.2vw, 4rem);
}
```
This direction lives or dies on the face and its settings. Display at tight tracking and leading; text sizes few and far apart (for example 15, 22, 34 and the display size). Use real typographic detail: proper quotes, balanced line breaks, hung punctuation where it helps.
The direction decides sizes and how many of them; the type system decides how this face is set. Where the two give different tracking or leading, use the values of the type system: they are tuned to this face.
The display size itself comes from the hero blueprint below.

## 4. Colour — Charcoal and sage
```css
:root {
  --bg: #1c1c1c;
  --surface: #262626;
  --ink: #f1f1ef;
  --muted: #9a9b96;
  --accent: #8fa68e;
  --accent-ink: #1c1c1c;
  --field-1: #c8c5ba;  --field-1-ink: #1c1c1c;
}
```
Charcoal holds the page with off-white text, and one section may flip to a pale concrete tone with dark text. Colour comes from the photographs; grey sage is used for the single button and the active state of a control.
Either one saturated ground for the whole page with a single ink, or paper and ink with one section inverted and one in the accent as a full field. Colour does the job images would have done.

## 5. Imagery
**This subject is software and there are no screenshots, so its pictures are its own interface.** Wherever this sheet says photograph, image or object, build a believable fragment of the product in HTML at real size (real labels, numbers that add up, one item hovered or selected), put the attribute `data-ui` on its root, and crop or scale it the way the blueprint treats a photograph: a full-bleed close crop, a tile, the fixed half of a split. Where a fragment makes no sense, use a flat colour field with type on it. Never a fake dashboard of grey bars, never a device frame. If the product works on something physical (ships, plants, buildings, a city), one real picture of that world can carry a section: find it with `node /home/xavrir/.agents/skills/design-taste-frontend/scripts/assets.mjs "<that world>"`, download it with `--get`, credit it on the page, and never let it pass for the product or its customers.

The first screen has no picture. Further down, real pictures of the subject's world (found with scripts/assets.mjs) arrive large and few: a full-bleed photograph between two type sections, a looping clip in one band, an archival image beside a list. Do not add icons, shapes, patterns or abstract graphics to compensate for anything.

**Floor (the inspector checks it):** at least four real pictures on the page, usually six to twelve; SVG drawings do not count. One moving picture where an honest clip exists: `node <skill>/scripts/assets.mjs --video "<query>"`, used as a muted loop with a poster frame. Whatever this direction says about pictures, it is carried out with real ones.

Optional atmosphere, two at most, from the "Sky and space" set (skies, clouds, stars and night skies). The page does not need them.
Order of preference for imagery: (1) the project's own photographs and product shots; (2) images you generate, if an image tool is
available; (3) real pictures of the subject's world found in the open collections (assets.mjs, downloaded and credited on the page);
(4) drawings of the subject in code; (5) these. They are real photographs (Unsplash licence, served by picsum.photos) of places, light and
texture. They are atmosphere, never a stand-in for a specific product, dish, person or interface. List the ones you use in your report so
they can be replaced.

- `https://picsum.photos/id/847/1600/1000` — A few birds flying across a pale blue gradient sky · light tone · pale blue, soft white, indigo · calm area: all · native landscape · photo: Andrej Chudy

Other sets that fit this brief: fog-and-quiet, water, architecture, texture — `node draw.mjs --photos <set>` prints one in full; `--photos list` shows all sets. Taking photographs from another set is your call and is not a redraw; keep them to one light and one treatment.

## 6. Hero — Colour field, two-ink statement
One saturated flat colour covers the whole viewport; there is no navigation bar and no image. Two short pieces of 15px text sit in the top corners on 3% margins: the wordmark at the left, the single action as a text link in the dark ink at the right. The statement starts 15% down, left-aligned from the 3% margin, in a soft heavy serif or a heavy grotesk at clamp(110px, 14vw, 220px), tracking -0.03em, leaded so tightly (0.82) that the two lines nearly touch. Line one is set in a pale ink and line two in a dark ink, the only two inks on the field; the pair fills the top 55% of the screen and the longer line reaches about 90% of the width. A long name may be broken across the two lines with a hyphen if the second half reads as a word of its own. In the bottom-right corner, right-aligned in a column 24% of the viewport wide, one supporting sentence at 17px in the pale ink. The bottom-left quarter stays empty colour.

On a phone: The same field. The corner texts stay at the top at 14px. The statement sets at 21vw on two lines from a 16px margin, starting at 16% of the height, pale line over dark line. The supporting sentence moves to the bottom-left at 16px across 80% of the width, ending 24px above the bottom edge. The middle fifth of the screen stays empty colour.
Headline: The name or a two-word statement over two lines, at most 8 characters per line at 21vw on a phone.

Adjusted for this page:
- It sets running text at 15px: paragraphs use your body step (16px or more).
- It names a kind of typeface: use the dealt faces (display Erode, text Erode) and their settings; capitals only where the type system sets them.
- It sets a sentence large over several lines: jurors read that as a long headline. Either cut it to a two-line headline at display size, or keep it a statement at 36px or less on three lines (24px, four lines on a phone).
- Its action is a text link: the primary action on the first screen is a real button (filled or solid-bordered, 44px or taller), placed where the blueprint puts the link. A second action may stay a link.

## 7. Sections, in order
Each is a composition to rebuild with this project's content. Keep the geometry; change the subject. Re-order or drop a
section when the argument of the page needs it (see references/copy.md), never to fall back to a card grid.

### 1. intro — Title, note, facts row (split)
First row: a heading of two to four words in a light weight at clamp(44px, 5vw, 76px) sits on the left margin in columns 1–6; a three-line paragraph at 16px, 260px wide, sits in columns 10–12 with its last line on the heading's baseline. Columns 7–9 stay empty. 120px lower, a second row sets three or four facts (a date, a time, a place, a price) as large light text at clamp(28px, 3vw, 44px), spread across the 12 columns with `justify-content: space-between`. Each fact has a one-line explanation at 14px directly under it in the soft ink, left-aligned to the fact. Facts are separated only by the space between them, so the row reads like the line of times on a printed programme. A photograph may start under the right half of the facts row, 5 columns wide at 4:3. Padding 140px top, 120px bottom.
On a phone: Heading at 11vw on the left margin; the note follows directly under it at 16px, full width. The facts become a two-column grid with a 32px row gap, each fact at 26px with its explanation beneath. Any photograph goes last, full width.

### 2. how-it-works — Call and response column (list)
The sequence is shown as what you give and what comes back, in one narrow column 560px wide starting at column 4, or on the page's text axis. Three exchanges, 88px apart. Each opens with a sentence at 20px medium that begins with its order word ('First, install it.' 'Then ask for a chart.'). 16px under it, the call: the literal thing the user enters, in a filled panel one tone off the ground, radius 8px, padding 20px. For a developer tool that is real code in `<pre><code>` at 14px with a working copy button; otherwise it is the product's own input holding real typed text. Under the call, indented 56px, the response: a true-size fragment marked data-ui showing what the product returns. The third response is the finished result and breaks out of the column, 760px wide, running to column 12. The heading, two to four words at 28px, sits 56px above the first sentence.
On a phone: The column fills the width with 20px margins. Calls and responses stack without the indent; a 24px gap and a lighter panel tone set the response apart. Code panels scroll sideways inside themselves. The final result runs edge to edge at true size, cropped on the right if wider.

Adjusted for this page:
- It names its own corners: use the one radius of this page (square unless the direction says otherwise) and one button shape.

### 3. showcase — Two giant words compared (statement)
No photographs: the thing shown is a pair of words, characters or figures that make the point by comparison (before and after, input and output, ours and the usual). At the top of the section, on one row: the heading, two to four words at 28–36px, at the left margin, and a paragraph of up to 60 words at 17px starting at column 7. Then 12vh of space. Below, two specimens side by side, one starting on column 1 and one on column 7, each set at clamp(180px, 34vw, 520px) with leading 0.8, so each fills about half the viewport's width. The section has a fixed height and overflow: hidden, so its bottom edge cuts both specimens at roughly 60% of their height. One short caption at 16px sits above the top-left of each specimen, naming it. Both specimens share one ink; the difference is in the letterforms or values, not in colour.
On a phone: Heading first, then the paragraph, at full width. The two specimens stack, each at 62vw type size in its own 38svh box with overflow hidden so the lower part is cut. Captions sit above each box at 15px. The second box takes the inverse colours of the first.

### 4. features — Comparison set as text (table)
A comparison table with no lines and no tick marks. A CSS grid: capability names in columns 1–5 of 12 at 22–24px medium, then three answer columns of equal width (plans, methods, or this against the usual way). Every cell answers in words at 17px: 'Yes, unlimited', 'Up to five', 'Not yet', never a symbol. Seven to ten rows, each 64–72px tall, with every other row on a ground one tone darker so the eye tracks across; rows touch. The column that matters is one filled block in the accent or ink colour running the full height of the table behind its cells and rising 24px above the first row, with its text reversed. Column names sit in the top row at 17px bold. The heading, two to four words at 40–48px, hangs top-left above column 1 with 48px beneath.
On a phone: The table turns on its side: one block per capability. The name at 20px bold, then three lines beneath, each giving a column name and its answer at 16px; the featured column's line is set on the filled colour at full width. Blocks alternate the two ground tones.

### 5. proof — Names as one sentence (statement)
Client, press or partner names are written out as one run-on sentence and set large: clamp(40px, 5vw, 76px), leading 1.0, flush left across all twelve columns, wrapping naturally over five to seven lines. Names are separated by commas and the list ends with a full stop. All names are set in a dimmed ink (still at least 3:1 against the ground), so the block reads as texture; one name at a time is lit in the full ink, and the lit name moves through the list every two seconds (a class toggled by a short script, with a 400ms colour transition). The sentence opens with two or three plain words in the full ink that act as the heading: the phrase that introduces the names. Nothing else is in the section: no logos, no columns, and 120px of space above and below.
On a phone: The same sentence at 9vw, wrapping over ten to fourteen lines; cut the list to the twelve strongest names so it stays within one and a half screens. The lit name still cycles. Margins are 20px and there are no other elements.

Adjusted for this page:
- It names a kind of typeface: use the dealt faces (display Erode, text Erode) and their settings; capitals only where the type system sets them.
- It sets a sentence large over several lines: jurors read that as a long headline. Either cut it to a two-line headline at display size, or keep it a statement at 36px or less on three lines (24px, four lines on a phone).

### 6. about — Side facts, wide paragraph (table)
Two rows share one vertical line at 34% of the viewport width. Row one: in a 160px column at the left margin, three label and value pairs stack with 20px between them, each a label in the soft ink with its value beneath in full ink, all at 13px (founded, based in, team size). From the 34% line a five-line paragraph at clamp(20px, 1.7vw, 26px), leading 1.35, runs to 90% of the width. Row two starts 120px lower and repeats the logic for the record of the practice: one plain word at 13px at the far left (awards, press, clients), then from the 34% line two list columns, each 260px wide with a 64px gutter, at 13px with 10px between lines: name at the left, year at the right in the soft ink. No heading; the paragraph opens the section. Each list comfortably holds 6–12 lines. No photographs are needed.
On a phone: Everything aligns to the left margin. The paragraph comes first at 19px. The three label and value pairs follow as a two-column grid at 14px. The two record lists stack with 40px between them, each opened by its plain word in full ink.

### 7. stats — One figure owns the screen (statement)
One number, one screen. A flat field at least 90svh tall. At the top-left, starting on the page's main axis, a one-line claim of two to five words at 36–44px: this is the heading. Then a long gap of 22–28vh. At the bottom-left on the same edge, a single figure at clamp(180px, 23vw, 380px), leading 0.8, tight tracking, with its unit (a percent sign, 'x', 'hrs') set as a superscript one third of the figure's size and aligned to the figure's cap height. Directly beneath, flush with the figure's left edge, a caption of three lines at 16–17px, 32ch wide, saying exactly what was measured and when. The right half of the field stays empty; resist filling it. The figure is static text in lining numerals and does not animate. A source line, if needed, sits at the bottom-right at 15px.
On a phone: The claim sits at the top at 28px. The figure is 46vw, flush left, with its superscript unit; if it has four characters or more, drop it to 34vw so it stays on one line. The caption follows at 16px across 80% of the width. Minimum height 80svh.

### 8. pricing — The price, very large (split)
One plan, no card. Columns 1–4 hold a heading of two to four words at 28–36px and one paragraph at 17px, top-aligned. Columns 6–12 hold the price as a display figure: numerals at clamp(120px, 16vw, 240px), tight tracking, leading 0.85, with the currency sign at half that size raised to the cap line and the period (per month, once) at 18px beside the last numeral on the baseline. One thing from the page overlaps the numerals by 10–15% of their height: the product photograph, the interface fragment or the motif shape, placed over the top-right of the figure. Beneath the figure, on its left edge, four plain lines at 18px state what is included, one per line, without ticks or bullets, then a single rectangular button. The section is at least 80svh tall with the figure vertically centred.
On a phone: The heading and paragraph come first, full width. The figure follows at 34vw, flush left, with the overlapping object tucked at its top-right corner at a third of the screen wide. Included lines at 17px and a full-width button close the block.

### 9. cta — A sentence with a blank (statement)
The closing sentence has a gap in it, and the gap is the form. A sentence of five to eight words is set at about 6vw on two lines, flush left across columns 1–11; there is no separate heading. Where the reader's email (or name, or city) belongs, an `<input>` sits inline on the baseline: a filled block of the one-step tone, exactly one line-height tall and about 7em wide, with the placeholder in the same face and size in the softer ink, no border, and the page's radius or motif corner. The sentence's last word is the submit `<button>`, set in the same type on a block of the accent colour of the same height. Field and button use `em` units so they scale with the sentence. One line at 16px under the sentence, in the softer ink, says what happens after sending. Nothing else is in the section.
On a phone: The sentence is set at 10vw on two lines; the input drops to a line of its own between them at full width and one line-height tall, and the final word, as the button, ends the second line. The explaining line follows at 15px.

Adjusted for this page:
- It sets a sentence large over several lines: jurors read that as a long headline. Either cut it to a two-line headline at display size, or keep it a statement at 36px or less on three lines (24px, four lines on a phone).

### 10. footer — Columns above, wordmark below (stack)
The footer inverts ground and ink and fills a whole viewport (min-height 100svh), arranged as three rows in a grid (auto 1fr auto). Top row, 40px from the edge: four small columns on the 12-column grid (address, navigation, social, hours or contact), each a plain list at 15–16px with its first line in the full ink and the rest in the softer ink. Middle row: empty. Bottom row: the wordmark alone, sized to run from the left margin to the right margin on one line. Set it as inline SVG text with a `viewBox` fitted to the word and `width: 100%`, or tune `font-size` in `vw` to the letter count, so it never wraps; its baseline sits 20px above a single line of legal text at 13px. With `position: sticky; bottom: 0` and a lower `z-index` than the page, the last section slides away to uncover it.
On a phone: Columns become two by two at 15px with 32px between rows, at the top. A gap of about 20svh follows. The wordmark spans the full width at the bottom; a name longer than ten letters stacks on two lines, each fitted to the width. Legal line beneath at 12px.

Adjusted for this page:
- It sets text at 12px: use your small step (13px or more) instead.

## 8. Motion — Colour by chapter
Each chapter is its own full-bleed field of one palette colour, so the ground changes exactly at section edges as you scroll, never as a global recolouring of the screen. The only movement: the fixed header (or the one pinned element) takes the ink of the chapter beneath it, switching with a 300ms colour transition as each edge passes under it. Do not drive the page background from an observer: in blind tests a page recoloured mid-section, with a paragraph sitting on two colours, read as broken twice.
This is the signature move, the one loud moment. Around it the page still needs the rest of the motion layer (SKILL.md step 9): an entrance for the parts of every section, feedback on every control, and one moving picture. The inspector fails a page that barely moves. Recipes: references/motion.md.

## 9. Where the richness comes from
Scale contrast and colour mass. A capability list set at 5vw is an image in its own right.

## 10. How this direction goes wrong
Without pictures, weak copy has nowhere to hide: write real sentences. Vary the set pieces; four sections of big-heading-plus-paragraph is a text document, not a design. Keep all headings within two lines.
