This is the direction for this build. Adopt it. Do not blend it with the layout you would have made anyway,
and do not redraw because it is unfamiliar: unfamiliar is the point. Redraw a slot only when it conflicts
with a stated brand constraint or needs an asset that does not exist, and say why in your report.

The parts of this sheet were written separately and will disagree in small ways. Settle every disagreement in this order:
**house rules** (including type no smaller than 13px and body text of 16px or more) → **the direction** (the idea, the composition habits, where the
one accent goes) → **the type system and the palette** (which face does which job, the five size steps, which colour is the ground and where the
accent goes) → **the hero blueprint** → **the section blueprints**. Under each blueprint below, "Adjusted for this page" lists where it disagrees.
In practice: take geometry from blueprints (columns, proportions, what sits where) and take everything else from above them. Map every size a
blueprint names onto one of your five type steps instead of adding a sixth. Use one button shape, one corner radius and one pair of page margins on
the whole page, whatever individual blueprints say. If a blueprint names a kind of typeface (a light serif, a heavy grotesk), use the dealt face.

## 1. The idea
Lay the page out like a well-designed printed report or magazine feature: a masthead image, a tight oversized title, text held to a strict column line, and colour used in solid blocks the way a two-colour print job would. It gives weight and authority to subjects that are really about content: findings, a manifesto, a programme, a serious product. The reader should feel that someone edited this.

Before anything else, write one sentence in your notes: "On this page, <the subject's own material> becomes <the device>."
Fill the first blank from your inventory of what only this client has (its name and what it means, its street, its numbers, its
constraint), then put the nearest competitor in the sentence: if it still works, the idea is the category's, not the client's.
Then name the one live thing the visitor can do on the page (SKILL.md step 4).

## 2. Composition
A fixed vertical axis at about 42% of the viewport width organises everything: headings begin on it, body columns hang from it, and the 0–42% zone holds small side notes, figures or stays empty. Masthead: a wide image band (photographic mosaic or a single crop) across the top 45% of the first screen; below it the title at 7–9vw starting on the axis, with a two-sentence standfirst at 20–24px. Sections alternate between text pages (title on the axis, body in one or two columns of 34–40ch to its right) and full-bleed colour pages carrying one large figure, quotation or image mosaic. Mosaics are gutterless: photographs and flat colour tiles butted together in a 4- or 6-cell grid. Page numbers, running heads and other print furniture are left out.

## 3. Type — Imbue / Albert Sans
A tall, condensed high-contrast serif whose capitals read like a masthead or a shop fascia. Suits residences, hotels, restaurants and wine, where a short name can stand floor to ceiling.
```html
<link rel="preconnect" href="https://fonts.googleapis.com">
<link rel="preconnect" href="https://fonts.gstatic.com" crossorigin>
<link href="https://fonts.googleapis.com/css2?family=Imbue:wght@400&family=Albert+Sans:wght@400;500&display=swap" rel="stylesheet">
```
- Display: **Imbue** — weight 400 with optical sizing on · tracking -0.01em · leading 0.9 · uppercase, sized so one short word fills its column (6–12vw)
- Text: **Albert Sans** — 400 for body, 500 for emphasis · leading 1.55 · tracking 0
```css
:root {
  --step-small: clamp(0.8125rem, 0.79rem + 0.1vw, 0.875rem);
  --step-body: clamp(1.0625rem, 1rem + 0.25vw, 1.1875rem);
  --step-lead: clamp(1.25rem, 1.1rem + 0.7vw, 1.625rem);
  --step-head: clamp(2rem, 1.3rem + 3.2vw, 4rem);
}
```
A tight heavy grotesk or a sharp serif for titles; an excellent text face for reading at 18–20px with leading near 1.5. Headings in sentence case, tracked tight.
The direction decides sizes and how many of them; the type system decides how this face is set. Where the two give different tracking or leading, use the values of the type system: they are tuned to this face.
The display size itself comes from the hero blueprint below.

```css
:root {

  --muted: #c77aa0;
```

Deep burgundy is the ground for the whole page and bubblegum pink sets every word on it. The button and the active state of a control are solid pink with burgundy type; product photographs supply any other colour.
Paper, ink and two or three solid block colours. A colour page is a whole section in one flat colour.

## 5. Imagery
**This subject is software and there are no screenshots, so its pictures are its own interface.** Wherever this sheet says photograph, image or object, build a believable fragment of the product in HTML at real size (real labels, numbers that add up, one item hovered or selected), put the attribute `data-ui` on its root, and crop or scale it the way the blueprint treats a photograph: a full-bleed close crop, a tile, the fixed half of a split. Where a fragment makes no sense, use a flat colour field with type on it. Never a fake dashboard of grey bars, never a device frame. If the product works on something physical (ships, plants, buildings, a city), one real picture of that world can carry a section: find it with `node /home/xavrir/.agents/skills/design-taste-frontend/scripts/assets.mjs "<that world>"`, download it with `--get`, credit it on the page, and never let it pass for the product or its customers.

Photographs used as blocks in mosaics alongside flat colour tiles; mixed subjects are fine if cropped tight and converted to one treatment.

**Floor (the inspector checks it):** at least four real pictures on the page, usually six to twelve; SVG drawings do not count. One moving picture where an honest clip exists: `node <skill>/scripts/assets.mjs --video "<query>"`, used as a muted loop with a poster frame. Whatever this direction says about pictures, it is carried out with real ones.

Optional atmosphere, two at most, from the "Sky and space" set (skies, clouds, stars and night skies). The page does not need them.
Order of preference for imagery: (1) the project's own photographs and product shots; (2) images you generate, if an image tool is
available; (3) real pictures of the subject's world found in the open collections (assets.mjs, downloaded and credited on the page);
(4) drawings of the subject in code; (5) these. They are real photographs (Unsplash licence, served by picsum.photos) of places, light and
texture. They are atmosphere, never a stand-in for a specific product, dish, person or interface. List the ones you use in your report so
they can be replaced.
Treatment: Give all photographs in a mosaic one treatment: greyscale via `filter: grayscale(1) contrast(1.05)`, or all full colour. Do not mix.
- `https://picsum.photos/id/901/1600/1000` — Green aurora sweeping across the night sky above snowy ground · dark tone · emerald green, dark teal, slate · native square · photo: Marcelo Quinan
- `https://picsum.photos/id/847/1600/1000` — A few birds flying across a pale blue gradient sky · light tone · pale blue, soft white, indigo · calm area: all · native landscape · photo: Andrej Chudy
- `https://picsum.photos/id/833/1600/1000` — Tiny aeroplane crossing a wide, softly overcast sky · light tone · warm grey, off-white · calm area: all · native portrait · photo: Paul Green
- `https://picsum.photos/id/505/1600/1000` — Tiny silhouetted figure on a dark ridge under a blue and orange twilight sky · dark tone · deep blue, orange, black · calm area: top · native landscape · photo: lee Scott
The last two path numbers are width and height: request the exact crop you render (`/1200/1500` for a 4:5 portrait, `/2400/1350` for a hero) and
add `?grayscale` to desaturate. Use at most two. Always set `width`, `height`, `alt` and `loading="lazy"` (not on the hero image).
Other sets that fit this brief: fog-and-quiet, water, architecture, texture — `node draw.mjs --photos <set>` prints one in full; `--photos list` shows all sets. Taking photographs from another set is your call and is not a redraw; keep them to one light and one treatment.

## 6. Hero — Void, then statement on axis
A deliberate void: the upper 60% of the viewport holds nothing but the top row. That row, 44px from the top on 48px margins, has the wordmark at the left margin, two text links starting exactly on a vertical axis at 42% of the width, and the single action at the right margin as a text link. Everything else hangs from the same axis in the bottom third. The statement starts on the 42% line: two lines at clamp(56px, 6.6vw, 104px), regular or medium weight, tracking -0.04em, leading 0.95, running towards the right margin, with its last baseline 72px above the bottom edge. Left of the axis, in columns 1-4, one supporting sentence at 18px in the soft ink, max 32ch, is bottom-aligned to the statement's last baseline. Nothing sits in the left zone above it. No image and no shape; the ground is one flat tone and the ink one colour. The emptiness is composed: three elements share the axis, so the blank area reads as intended and not as a page that failed to load.

On a phone: The axis collapses to the 20px left margin. The top 48% of the screen stays empty under the wordmark row. The statement sets at 12.5vw on two lines, flush left, followed by the sentence at 17px and the action as a text link, the whole block ending 28px above the bottom edge. The two links fold behind a menu word.
Headline: A 3-6 word statement, at most 30 characters over two lines, about 15 per line at 6.6vw from the axis.

Adjusted for this page:
- It sets a sentence large over several lines: jurors read that as a long headline. Either cut it to a two-line headline at display size, or keep it a statement at 36px or less on three lines (24px, four lines on a phone).
- Its action is a text link: the primary action on the first screen is a real button (filled or solid-bordered, 44px or taller), placed where the blueprint puts the link. A second action may stay a link.

## 7. Sections, in order
Each is a composition to rebuild with this project's content. Keep the geometry; change the subject. Re-order or drop a
section when the argument of the page needs it (see references/copy.md), never to fall back to a card grid.

### 1. intro — Lead line over four columns (grid)
Two parts on one 12-column grid with 20px margins. First, a lead paragraph of 30–40 words spans all 12 columns at clamp(22px, 2.2vw, 32px), regular weight, leading 1.2: three lines that read as the opening of a text, with no heading before it. 72px below, four equal columns (three grid columns each, 24px gutters) carry small text at 14–15px with leading 1.45. Each column is one paragraph of 40–70 words that opens with a bold run-in phrase of two or three words in full ink, the rest continuing on the same line in the soft ink. Columns top-align and end ragged at different depths. No cards, icons or dividers; the bold run-ins are the only markers. The four columns answer four plain questions: who, what, where, how to reach them. Padding is modest, 96px top and bottom, so a sheet of images can start straight after.
On a phone: The lead paragraph at 20px, full width. The four columns become a two-by-two grid with a 20px gutter and 40px row gap at 14px; if any paragraph exceeds 60 words, switch to one column with 32px between paragraphs. Run-in phrases stay bold.

### 2. how-it-works — Stages as one bar (band)
The product's progress bar, enlarged until it spans the page, is the diagram. One bar runs across columns 1–12, 22vh tall, built as a flex row of three or four solid segments with 6px gaps, root marked data-ui. Each segment's width follows how long that stage really takes (flex-grow from the true durations, 12% minimum), and each is a different flat palette colour, so the reader sees at once that signing up is a sliver and the rest is the product working. Inside every segment, top-left with 20px padding, its duration in words at clamp(20px, 2vw, 30px): 'Two minutes', 'About a day'. Under each segment, on its left edge, one clause at 17px, 22ch wide, beginning with its order word. Above the bar, 64px clear of it, the heading: two to five words at clamp(48px, 6.4vw, 104px), flush left. Nothing else.
On a phone: The bar turns vertical: segments stack as full-width blocks whose heights keep the proportions, 72px minimum, 6px gaps, about 70svh in total. Each holds its duration at 22px top-left and its clause at 16px beneath. The heading sits above at 11vw on two lines.

### 3. showcase — Rows as a board (table)
The product's main list is set like a station departure board. Eight to ten real rows (transactions, issues, deploys, bookings) run the full viewport width inside 12px side margins. Each row is a filled band 72px tall, one tone off a dark ground, separated from the next by a 4px gap and never by a line; the whole board is one fragment marked data-ui. Text inside is 26–30px: the name from column 1, two or three facts at columns 6, 8 and 10, and at column 12 a status word on a solid block of its status colour; figures use tabular numerals. The section's top and bottom edges cut the first and last rows in half, so the list seems to continue. The fourth row is open: 216px tall, in the accent colour, with its detail in two lines at 17px. Clicking another row opens that one instead. No heading: the open row's name is the largest type.
On a phone: Rows keep the 4px gaps and are 64px tall inside 8px margins: the name at 20px on the left, the status block on the right, the other facts dropped. The open row is 200px tall and lists the dropped facts as three lines at 16px. Six rows show; the first and last are cropped.

Adjusted for this page:
- It sets text at 12px: use your small step (13px or more) instead.

### 4. features — Panel with margin notes (index)
One tall panel from the product is shown whole, like a plate in a manual, with notes in both margins. The panel (an inspector, a sidebar, a composer) is built at true size, 360–400px wide and 640–720px tall, root marked data-ui, and stands in columns 5–8 on the plain ground with no stage behind it. Six notes describe six of its parts: three in columns 1–4, right-aligned, and three in columns 9–12, left-aligned, each 24ch wide and placed by absolute top offsets so its first line is level with the control it explains. A note is a name at 18px medium and two lines at 15px in the soft ink. Hovering or focusing a note keeps its control at full strength and drops the rest of the panel to 30% opacity; the controls themselves also work. The heading, two to four words at 32–40px, sits right-aligned in columns 1–4, level with the panel's top edge.
On a phone: The panel is shown in three slices, top, middle and bottom, each at true size and centred, cut where the next begins. Under each slice its two notes sit side by side in two columns at 15px. 56px between slices. The heading leads at 26px on the left margin.

Adjusted for this page:
- It fades text: anything a visitor reads, inactive items of a list included, is `--ink` or `--muted` at full strength (contrast 4.5:1 or more). The active item is told apart by `--ink` against `--muted`, weight or the accent, not by fading the rest.

### 5. proof — Recognition as a table (table)
Awards, press mentions or credits set as a four-column table with no lines. The heading is two or three words at about 5vw, stepped over two lines with the second line indented by two columns, and occupies columns 1–7. The table begins 72px below and spans columns 1–12 on a fixed grid: year in columns 1–2, project or title in 3–6, awarding body or publication in 7–10, the award itself in 11–12, right-aligned. Rows are set at 16–17px with 14px of padding above and below; every second row carries a background tone one step from the ground, running the full table width, which replaces rules. Year groups are separated by one blank row, and the year is printed only on the first row of its group. Use a real `<table>` with `border-collapse: collapse` and no borders.
On a phone: The heading drops to 11vw. Each table row becomes a two-line block: title on the first line in the full ink, then body and award on the second in the softer ink. The year stands once as a 22px figure above its group. Alternate blocks keep the background tone.

Adjusted for this page:
- It draws lines or borders: keep them only inside a table, a form or a `data-ui` fragment; elsewhere separate with space or a change of ground.

### 6. about — Wide photo, colour tile, facts (mosaic)
Top: a heading of two to four words at clamp(40px, 4vw, 60px) on the left margin, then 24px below it a three-line lead paragraph at 21–22px across columns 1–6. 72px lower, two tiles sit side by side with a 16px gap. The left tile spans columns 1–8: a landscape photograph at 16:10 with one radius (0 or 10px). A short paragraph of up to 35 words at 16px is set in a solid paper-colour block 320px wide with 20px padding, placed over the photograph's top-right corner and flush with both edges. The right tile spans columns 9–12 and is a flat palette colour, 60% of the photograph's height: it holds a simple inline SVG outline of the region or the floor plan as one filled shape, with one solid dot marking the location. Under the colour tile, four label and value pairs at 14px in a two-by-two grid: altitude, distance, season, local time.
On a phone: Heading at 9vw, lead at 18px. The photograph goes full width at 4:3 and its overlaid paragraph moves beneath it as ordinary text. The colour tile follows at full width and 3:2, and the four label and value pairs sit under it in two columns.

Adjusted for this page:
- It sets text at 10px: use your small step (13px or more) instead.

### 7. stats — One figure owns the screen (statement)
One number, one screen. A flat field at least 90svh tall. At the top-left, starting on the page's main axis, a one-line claim of two to five words at 36–44px: this is the heading. Then a long gap of 22–28vh. At the bottom-left on the same edge, a single figure at clamp(180px, 23vw, 380px), leading 0.8, tight tracking, with its unit (a percent sign, 'x', 'hrs') set as a superscript one third of the figure's size and aligned to the figure's cap height. Directly beneath, flush with the figure's left edge, a caption of three lines at 16–17px, 32ch wide, saying exactly what was measured and when. The right half of the field stays empty; resist filling it. The figure is static text in lining numerals and does not animate. A source line, if needed, sits at the bottom-right at 15px.
On a phone: The claim sits at the top at 28px. The figure is 46vw, flush left, with its superscript unit; if it has four characters or more, drop it to 34vw so it stays on one line. The caption follows at 16px across 80% of the width. Minimum height 80svh.

### 8. pricing — Plans as a large table (table)
Two to four plans compared in a table whose type is far larger than a table usually gets. The first column (columns 1–4) lists what is compared, in words, at 22px. Each plan takes an equal share of columns 5–12. The header row gives each plan's name at about 3vw bold, left-aligned, with its price directly beneath at the same size in the regular weight and the period at 16px. Body rows are 22px with 20px padding; cells answer in words or numbers (“Unlimited”, “3 seats”, “Not included”), never with ticks. Every second row has a background tone one step from the ground, spanning all columns. The recommended plan is marked only by its whole column sitting on the accent tone from header to last row. The final row holds one rectangular button per plan. A two-to-four-word heading sits in the top-left cell.
On a phone: Plans stack as separate blocks. Each opens with the plan name and price at 9vw, then its rows as pairs: the compared item in the softer ink, the answer beneath in the full ink, both at 17px. The recommended plan's block sits on the accent tone. A full-width button ends each block.

### 9. cta — Three photographs, then a field (strip)
Two parts with no gap between them. First, a strip of three photographs butted edge to edge across the full viewport, each one third wide and 40svh tall, `object-fit: cover`, no gutters and no captions; give all three the same treatment. Directly beneath, a band in one flat palette colour, about 44svh tall. In the band, columns 1–7: the closing statement at 26–30px over three lines in the band's assigned ink, with exactly one phrase underlined by a 4px bar (`text-decoration-thickness: 4px; text-underline-offset: 6px`), and that phrase is the link; two lines at 16px follow 32px lower. In the lower-right corner, columns 11–12, a small portrait photograph (4:5, about 150px wide) sits 40px from the band's edges: the person who will answer. There is no button; the underlined phrase is the action.
On a phone: The strip becomes one full-width photograph at 4:3 with the other two side by side beneath it at half width each, still gutterless. The band follows: statement at 22px, the two small lines, and the portrait at 96px wide in the bottom-right corner.

Adjusted for this page:
- It draws lines or borders: keep them only inside a table, a form or a `data-ui` fragment; elsewhere separate with space or a change of ground.

### 10. footer — Name first, places under it (list)
The wordmark opens the footer instead of closing it. On a full-bleed field in the page's inverse or final chapter colour, the name spans the full content width as the first thing, its cap line pushed 8% above the footer's top edge so the tops of the letters are sliced off by the section (`overflow: hidden` and a negative top margin in `em`). 72px beneath, a two-part row: columns 1–5 list the places or ways to reach the studio (cities, or email and telephone), one per line at 32–36px in the accent ink, each a link; columns 7–12 hold three columns of small links at 15px, top-aligned with the first place. A final row 96px lower carries the legal line at the left and a back-to-top text link at the right, both 13px. The field is about 90svh tall, and nothing sits between the wordmark and the row but space.
On a phone: The sliced wordmark stays at the top, fitted to the width. Places follow as a list at 26px, then the link columns two across at 15px. Legal line and back-to-top link share the last row at 12px, at opposite margins.

Adjusted for this page:
- It sets text at 12px: use your small step (13px or more) instead.
- It crops letters along their top: the inspector fails that (jurors read it as broken). Crop at the bottom edge or a side instead.

## 8. Motion — Masked headline
On load, each line of the headline rises from behind its own baseline, about 900ms with a strong ease-out, the second line 110ms after the first; the supporting sentence and the action then fade up together. Nothing else on the page performs. Because headlines are two lines at most, you author the line breaks and wrap each line yourself. Recipe: 'Masked line reveal' in references/motion.md.
This is the signature move, the one loud moment. Around it the page still needs the rest of the motion layer (SKILL.md step 9): an entrance for the parts of every section, feedback on every control, and one moving picture. The inspector fails a page that barely moves. Recipes: references/motion.md.

## 9. Where the richness comes from
The column line, the mosaics and whole pages of colour give structure and variety without ornaments.

## 10. How this direction goes wrong
The axis must be exact and reused everywhere or it reads as random indentation. Keep paragraphs to a real reading measure. On a phone the axis collapses to the left margin and side notes move inline above their paragraph.
