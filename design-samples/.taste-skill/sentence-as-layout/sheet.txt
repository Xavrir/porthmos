This is the direction for this build. Adopt it. Do not blend it with the layout you would have made anyway,
and do not redraw because it is unfamiliar: unfamiliar is the point. Redraw a slot only when it conflicts
with a stated brand constraint or needs an asset that does not exist, and say why in your report.

The parts of this sheet were written separately and will disagree in small ways. Settle every disagreement in this order:
**house rules** (including type no smaller than 13px and body text of 16px or more) → **the direction** (the idea, the composition habits, where the
one accent goes) → **the type system and the palette** (which face does which job, the five size steps, which colour is the ground and where the
accent goes) → **the hero blueprint** → **the section blueprints**. Under each blueprint below, "Adjusted for this page" lists where it disagrees.
In practice: take geometry from blueprints (columns, proportions, what sits where) and take everything else from above them. Map every size a
blueprint names onto one of your five type steps instead of adding a sixth. Use one button shape, one corner radius and one pair of page margins on
the whole page, whatever individual blueprints say. If a blueprint names a kind of typeface (a light serif, a heavy grotesk), use the dealt face.

## 1. The idea
One short sentence, set at poster scale, is the composition. The trick that makes it an idea rather than a big headline: something from the subject sits inside the sentence where a word would be. A strip of photograph fills the gap between two words; a small live fragment of the product replaces the noun; the object the sentence names is pictured at the size of a letter. Write the sentence first, find the word that can be shown instead of said, and put the showing in its place. The reader completes the sentence with their eyes.

Before anything else, write one sentence in your notes: "On this page, <the subject's own material> becomes <the device>."
Fill the first blank from your inventory of what only this client has (its name and what it means, its street, its numbers, its
constraint), then put the nearest competitor in the sentence: if it still works, the idea is the category's, not the client's.
Then name the one live thing the visitor can do on the page (SKILL.md step 4).

## 2. Composition
The sentence is four to seven words and runs as one or two full-width lines at 9–13vw. Inline with the type, on the baseline, sits one media slot: a rounded-rectangle or square frame exactly one line-height tall and two to three ems wide, holding a photograph or a looping crop. Above and below the sentence the screen is empty except for a small navigation line and one supporting sentence set small, both on the same left margin. Later sections repeat the grammar at smaller scale: each is a short statement on the left six columns with its proof (image, figure, list) on the right, so the page reads as a series of sentences with evidence. Generous vertical space (160px or more) between sections.

## 3. Type — Libre Franklin / Source Serif 4
An American gothic for headings over a sober transitional serif for reading, the pairing of a newspaper front page. Suits essays, annual reports, archives and finance pages that have real paragraphs.
```html
<link rel="preconnect" href="https://fonts.googleapis.com">
<link rel="preconnect" href="https://fonts.gstatic.com" crossorigin>
<link href="https://fonts.googleapis.com/css2?family=Libre+Franklin:wght@700&family=Source+Serif+4:ital,wght@0,400;0,600;1,400;1,600&display=swap" rel="stylesheet">
```
- Display: **Libre Franklin** — weight 700 · tracking -0.035em · leading 0.92 · uppercase for the title, sentence case for section headings
- Text: **Source Serif 4** — 400 for body, 600 for emphasis, italic for quotations · leading 1.55 · tracking 0
```css
:root {
  --step-small: clamp(0.8125rem, 0.79rem + 0.1vw, 0.875rem);
  --step-body: clamp(1.0625rem, 1rem + 0.25vw, 1.1875rem);
  --step-lead: clamp(1.25rem, 1.1rem + 0.7vw, 1.625rem);
  --step-head: clamp(2rem, 1.3rem + 3.2vw, 4rem);
}
```
Display weight with real presence (medium to bold), tracking -0.03 to -0.05em, leading 0.95–1.0 so two lines sit close. The inline frame aligns to cap height and baseline exactly; set it with em units so it scales with the type.
The direction decides sizes and how many of them; the type system decides how this face is set. Where the two give different tracking or leading, use the values of the type system: they are tuned to this face.
The display size itself comes from the hero blueprint below.

## 5. Imagery
**This subject is software and there are no screenshots, so its pictures are its own interface.** Wherever this sheet says photograph, image or object, build a believable fragment of the product in HTML at real size (real labels, numbers that add up, one item hovered or selected), put the attribute `data-ui` on its root, and crop or scale it the way the blueprint treats a photograph: a full-bleed close crop, a tile, the fixed half of a split. Where a fragment makes no sense, use a flat colour field with type on it. Never a fake dashboard of grey bars, never a device frame. If the product works on something physical (ships, plants, buildings, a city), one real picture of that world can carry a section: find it with `node /home/xavrir/.agents/skills/design-taste-frontend/scripts/assets.mjs "<that world>"`, download it with `--get`, credit it on the page, and never let it pass for the product or its customers.

One strong, simple photograph that reads at small size (a face, an object, a texture, a horizon), cropped to a wide strip inside the sentence. The same image may open to full width in the next section, so the small slot feels like a window onto something larger.

**Floor (the inspector checks it):** at least four real pictures on the page, usually six to twelve; SVG drawings do not count. One moving picture where an honest clip exists: `node <skill>/scripts/assets.mjs --video "<query>"`, used as a muted loop with a poster frame. Whatever this direction says about pictures, it is carried out with real ones.

Optional atmosphere, two at most, from the "Fog and quiet" set (misty, muted, minimal landscapes and empty horizons; calm areas for text). The page does not need them.
Order of preference for imagery: (1) the project's own photographs and product shots; (2) images you generate, if an image tool is
available; (3) real pictures of the subject's world found in the open collections (assets.mjs, downloaded and credited on the page);
(4) drawings of the subject in code; (5) these. They are real photographs (Unsplash licence, served by picsum.photos) of places, light and
texture. They are atmosphere, never a stand-in for a specific product, dish, person or interface. List the ones you use in your report so
they can be replaced.

- `https://picsum.photos/id/300/1600/1000` — Hay bales scattered across a pale field under white sky · light tone · white, light grey · black-and-white · calm area: top · native landscape · photo: Pavel Voinov

Other sets that fit this brief: sky-and-space, water, architecture, texture — `node draw.mjs --photos <set>` prints one in full; `--photos list` shows all sets. Taking photographs from another set is your call and is not a redraw; keep them to one light and one treatment.

## 6. Hero — Headline across two fields
The viewport is two flat fields meeting at exactly half height: paper above, one saturated colour below, with a hard edge and nothing drawn on it. A two-line headline in black-weight capitals at clamp(96px, 9.5vw, 150px), line-height 0.86, is centred across the seam and spans about 70% of the width, so line one stands on the upper field and line two is cut through its upper third by the seam. The type changes ink where it crosses: above the seam it takes the accent ink, below it a deep ink two steps darker than the lower field, as if submerged. Build it as two stacked copies of the same headline, one per ink, the first clipped with `clip-path: inset(0 0 50% 0)` and the second with `inset(50% 0 0 0)`. Wordmark top-left at 28px; up to four text links and the single action, a solid rectangular button, top-right. One supporting sentence at 20px in the paper colour is centred on the lower field, 56px above the bottom edge. The lower field continues for 30svh past the fold before the next colour begins.

On a phone: The same two fields with the seam at 46svh. The headline stays on two lines at 15vw, centred, still cut by the seam and still changing ink. Wordmark top-left and the action button top-right; the links drop into one menu word beside it. The supporting sentence sits on the lower field at 16px, centred, max 28ch, with 40px beneath it.
Headline: A statement of two to four words on two lines, up to about 10 characters per line, whose meaning the rising field acts out.

Adjusted for this page:
- It names a kind of typeface: use the dealt faces (display Libre Franklin, text Source Serif 4) and their settings; capitals only where the type system sets them.
- It names colours (black): map them onto this palette's roles (ground `--bg`, panels `--surface` and the fields, text `--ink`/`--muted`, the accent `--accent` on the primary action only).

## 7. Sections, in order
Each is a composition to rebuild with this project's content. Keep the geometry; change the subject. Re-order or drop a
section when the argument of the page needs it (see references/copy.md), never to fall back to a card grid.

### 1. intro — Statement with inline thumbnails (statement)
A centred statement of 18–28 words, four lines at clamp(32px, 4.2vw, 62px), medium or bold weight, leading 1.12, in a block 60–66% of the viewport wide with 22vh of empty ground above and below. Three to five small frames sit inline between words: each is an `inline-block` 0.85em tall and 0.85–1.3em wide, `vertical-align: -0.1em`, radius 0.14em, holding a tightly cropped photograph (or a crop of real interface) of the thing the neighbouring word names. Frames are part of the line, never floating over it, with 0.12em of space either side. No heading above and no paragraph below; the statement is set as a `p`. Directly under the section the page may split into two flat colour halves or begin the first product row, so the thumbnails read as a preview of what follows. On a motif-driven page every frame takes the motif's shape.
On a phone: Left-aligned instead of centred, at 8vw with leading 1.15, about seven lines inside 20px margins. Keep at most three inline frames, each exactly one cap-height tall, and make sure none lands at the start of a line. Vertical padding drops to 96px.

Adjusted for this page:
- It sets a sentence large over several lines: jurors read that as a long headline. Either cut it to a two-line headline at display size, or keep it a statement at 36px or less on three lines (24px, four lines on a phone).

### 2. how-it-works — Stages as one bar (band)
The product's progress bar, enlarged until it spans the page, is the diagram. One bar runs across columns 1–12, 22vh tall, built as a flex row of three or four solid segments with 6px gaps, root marked data-ui. Each segment's width follows how long that stage really takes (flex-grow from the true durations, 12% minimum), and each is a different flat palette colour, so the reader sees at once that signing up is a sliver and the rest is the product working. Inside every segment, top-left with 20px padding, its duration in words at clamp(20px, 2vw, 30px): 'Two minutes', 'About a day'. Under each segment, on its left edge, one clause at 17px, 22ch wide, beginning with its order word. Above the bar, 64px clear of it, the heading: two to five words at clamp(48px, 6.4vw, 104px), flush left. Nothing else.
On a phone: The bar turns vertical: segments stack as full-width blocks whose heights keep the proportions, 72px minimum, 6px gaps, about 70svh in total. Each holds its duration at 22px top-left and its clause at 16px beneath. The heading sits above at 11vw on two lines.

### 3. showcase — Headline you can type (statement)
The section's headline is an input. One line at clamp(48px, 8vw, 132px), tight tracking, flush left across columns 1–12, is a real `<input>` (root marked data-ui) with no border and no fill, prefilled with a believable two-to-five-word example of what a user gives the product: a search, a question, a date in words, a colour name. A solid caret block 0.08em wide blinks after the last character. 48px beneath, on the same left edge, the product's answer appears at true interface size, at most 560px wide: three result rows, one parsed value, or a swatch, updating as the visitor types. Bottom-left, three more examples at 17px act as plain text buttons that refill the line. The rest of the 88svh field is empty. If the text outgrows the line it scrolls inside the input; it never wraps.
On a phone: The input is set at 11vw on one line and scrolls sideways inside itself when the text runs long. The answer follows 28px below at full width and true size. The three examples become a wrapping row of filled blocks 44px tall beneath the answer; tapping one refills the line.

### 4. features — Rows with margin photographs (list)
Everything hangs from one vertical axis at column 5, about 35% of the width. First the heading: a statement of up to five words at clamp(40px, 5vw, 80px) starting on the axis, vertically centred in an otherwise empty 70svh of ground. Then four or five benefit rows, each starting on the same axis: a title at clamp(28px, 3vw, 48px) and under it a paragraph of 34ch at 17px. Columns 1–4 stay empty. At the right margin each row carries one photograph, top-aligned with its title and deliberately unequal: 240px wide at 4:5 in one row, 320px at 3:2 in the next, 180px square after that. Rows are separated by 96–120px of space, not lines. On a saturated field, set all type in one pale ink and leave the photographs ungraded.
On a phone: The axis collapses to the left margin. The statement sits at 9vw in 50svh of empty ground. Each row stacks its title at 26px, the paragraph, then its photograph at alternating widths: 100%, 70% flush right, 55% flush left. 64px between rows.

### 5. proof — Names as one sentence (statement)
Client, press or partner names are written out as one run-on sentence and set large: clamp(40px, 5vw, 76px), leading 1.0, flush left across all twelve columns, wrapping naturally over five to seven lines. Names are separated by commas and the list ends with a full stop. All names are set in a dimmed ink (still at least 3:1 against the ground), so the block reads as texture; one name at a time is lit in the full ink, and the lit name moves through the list every two seconds (a class toggled by a short script, with a 400ms colour transition). The sentence opens with two or three plain words in the full ink that act as the heading: the phrase that introduces the names. Nothing else is in the section: no logos, no columns, and 120px of space above and below.
On a phone: The same sentence at 9vw, wrapping over ten to fourteen lines; cut the list to the twelve strongest names so it stays within one and a half screens. The lit name still cycles. Margins are 20px and there are no other elements.

### 6. about — Portrait as drop cap (statement)
No heading. One biography paragraph of 50–80 words is set large at clamp(24px, 2.5vw, 38px), regular weight, leading 1.2, across columns 3–12 of the grid. A square portrait is floated at the paragraph's start with `float: left`: its side equals three lines of text (3.6em), with 0.5em of space to its right, so the first three lines wrap beside it and the fourth returns to the paragraph's left edge, like a drop capital. Square corners. The person's name, the first words of the paragraph, is in full ink; the rest may be in a softer ink with linked names in full ink and underlined. A second paragraph follows after one blank line in the soft ink at the same size. Columns 1–2 hold three short facts (place, since when, how to reach) at 14px, top-aligned with the portrait. Section padding 140px.
On a phone: Text at 22px with leading 1.25, full width. The portrait keeps its float but grows to four lines tall, about 110px square. The three facts move below the second paragraph as a single row of three small blocks at 13px.

### 7. stats — Column aligned on the decimal (table)
A tall column of figures set like a ledger. Left, columns 1–5: the heading as a statement of up to five words on two lines at clamp(32px, 3.2vw, 52px), its first line in the soft ink and its second in full ink, placed at mid-height. Right, columns 7–12: four to six figures stacked, each at clamp(64px, 7vw, 112px), leading 1.0, in tabular lining numerals. They align on the decimal point: each row is a three-part grid in which the whole-number part is right-aligned in the first track, the point and decimals are left-aligned in the second, and a caption of one or two lines at 16px sits in the third, on the figure's baseline. Whole numbers simply leave the second track empty. Rows are separated by 20px of space only. Units are written in the captions, not attached to the figures, so the numerals form one clean vertical shape.
On a phone: The heading comes first at 28px. Figures drop to 15vw and keep the decimal alignment, with the split at 52% of the width. Each caption moves under its figure at 15px, starting from the left margin. 28px between rows.

### 9. cta — A sentence with a blank (statement)

### 10. footer — Invitation over a cropped name (statement)

## 8. Motion — Colour by chapter
Each chapter is its own full-bleed field of one palette colour, so the ground changes exactly at section edges as you scroll, never as a global recolouring of the screen. The only movement: the fixed header (or the one pinned element) takes the ink of the chapter beneath it, switching with a 300ms colour transition as each edge passes under it. Do not drive the page background from an observer: in blind tests a page recoloured mid-section, with a paragraph sitting on two colours, read as broken twice.
This is the signature move, the one loud moment. Around it the page still needs the rest of the motion layer (SKILL.md step 9): an entrance for the parts of every section, feedback on every control, and one moving picture. The inspector fails a page that barely moves. Recipes: references/motion.md.

## 9. Where the richness comes from
The collision of big type and one live image inside it. Because there is only one image on the first screen, choose it with care and give it perfect alignment to the letters.

## 10. How this direction goes wrong
The sentence must be short enough for two lines on a phone, where the inline frame drops to its own line between the two halves. Do not style one word differently to fake the device. Do not use more than one slot per sentence.

## Always
Headlines two lines or fewer at every width. No eyebrow labels, no monospace, no 01/02/03, no thin rules, no gradients, no shadows at rest,
no cream-and-terracotta, no violet. `inspect.mjs` measures all of it.
