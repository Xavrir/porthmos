An importer in Singapore orders 100,000 USDC of chilled seafood from a supplier abroad. The buyer will not pay before the goods arrive; the supplier will not ship without seeing the money. Porthmos holds the money between them.

The importer. The finance or treasury manager locks the order, sees every leg and every proof, and approves what the agent proposes. Payroll and supplier bills stay covered first, because money that is owed is kept out of every investment.

The supplier. Sees in a free portal that the money is there before shipping, submits the proof for each leg, and watches each one pay out to the address registered for this contract and to no other.

The agent. Keeps cash for what is owed, invests only free cash in vaults the owner approved, and can only propose. Fixed code checks every proposal before anything is signed, and the contract enforces the result.

The bank. A risk officer sets limits per client, approves escalations and can freeze or revoke any agent. A freeze stops new deposits; exits back to the client's own Safe keep running, and only the bank can lift its freeze.

The harbour's own record: Singapore's docks and strait as museums and archives keep them. The crossing Porthmos guards is the same water.

Every crossing already leaves paper behind: a chart, a dock register, a bill of lading.

Porthmos pays when that paper checks out.

The crossing, in three legs

  1. 20,000 USDC

    First, the carrier's eBL is verified.The carrier signs at Cat Lai; Porthmos checks the signature.

  2. 40,000 USDC

    Then the reefer log holds 0 to 4 °C on arrival.The container's sensor log is signed at Pasir Panjang.

  3. 40,000 USDC

    Last, the quality report is accepted.Your surveyor signs; the final leg pays out.

Bad proof holds the next leg. The money stays locked, the supplier sees why, and you decide what happens next. The split and the proofs are the TradeFlow design; the milestone module is not built yet.
